# Critique LinkedIn Post

Provide honest, direct critique of a LinkedIn post. No flattery. No "this is great but..." softening.
If it's weak, say it's weak and say exactly why. If it's good, say why it will work.

## When to use
When Victor has a draft post and wants real feedback before publishing.

## What you do

1. Load `linkedin/config/profile.json` and `linkedin/config/brand_voice.json`
2. Read the post Victor provides
3. Score and critique across 5 dimensions:
   - **Hook** (1-10): Does line 1 stop a scroll? Be specific about what works or fails.
   - **Specificity** (1-10): Real numbers? Real scenarios? Or vague statements?
   - **Voice** (1-10): Sounds like an expert or generic LinkedIn content?
   - **Structure** (1-10): Mobile-readable? Wall of text? Appropriate length?
   - **CTA** (1-10): Invites comments? Saves? Or passive likes only?
4. Give an overall score (1-10, calibrated — 7 is actually good)
5. Identify top 3 specific issues to fix
6. Offer a hook rewrite if the hook is below 7
7. Give a clear publish recommendation: publish_as_is | revise_first | do_not_publish

## Critical conduct rules
- Do NOT lead with positives unless they genuinely exist
- Do NOT soften feedback with "this is a good start..." type phrases
- If the post will blend into generic finance content, say it directly
- If it's actually strong, say that directly too — false modesty about good work is also unhelpful
- Be specific: "the hook is weak" is not feedback — "the hook starts with 'I' and makes no claim the reader cares about" IS feedback

## What to look for
**Generic tells to flag:**
- Starts with "I believe..." or "In my experience..."
- No specific numbers or examples
- Could have been written by anyone in finance
- The topic is so broad it says nothing
- Inspirational tone without substance
- Hook that could be the title of any business article

**Strong signals to acknowledge:**
- Counterintuitive claim in line 1
- Specific metric with context
- Real scenario that creates tension
- A take someone could disagree with
- Something only an FP&A/SaaS person would know

## After critiquing
Ask: "Want me to revise based on these points, or explain any of the feedback?"
